Files that stop while the sound plays: new in build 0.4.5

The reverb engine's model had only ever learned from sounds that ring out inside the file. A file that stops while the sound still plays, or an excerpt cut at both ends, gives it no tail to read, and there it did as much harm as good: on 40 test reverbs cut off at 6 seconds its own answer made 48 of 100 worse than leaving them alone. Build 0.4.4 kept most of that off the screen, because its look with silence after the sound hid the REVERB group on about half of such files.

The model has now learned again, from 3,615 new examples. Each one is there in three forms: ringing out, cut off at 6 seconds, and as a 4.5 second excerpt. Over half of them have another effect on the sound before the reverb, and 45 of 100 have no reverb at all, so that it also learns where there is nothing to find. The Sureness figure on the screen was fitted again on 2,470 test sounds, and the look with silence is gone, because the new model does not need it.

A reverb that is not there

I looked at where the false REVERB of build 0.4.4 came from. On 912 test sounds with one other effect and no reverb it showed REVERB on 134. On 86 of those the ring is in the sound before any effect: recorded loops and sampled instruments (plucked strings, steel drum, vibraphone, choir) that ring by themselves or carry the room they were recorded in. The app cannot know that nobody added that ring later. The other 48 were made by the effect: an echo, a delay between left and right, a chorus or a widening can look like a room.

For that second kind the app now looks twice. When it finds a room together with an effect it knows how to take out (echoes, tremolo, auto pan, gate, flanger, phaser, filter sweep, chorus, a delay between left and right), it takes that effect out and looks for the room again, and the less sure of the two answers counts. Together with the new model that brings the false REVERB made by an effect from 48 to 26, and the kind that is in the sound itself from 86 to 67. Of 340 sounds with a real reverb, 78 were looked at again and one lost its REVERB group.

All told, on 577 test sounds without reverb that neither the model nor the Sureness figure was fitted on, REVERB is shown on 9 of 100, where build 0.4.4 showed it on 14. On the 136 scoreboard cases it is shown where there is none on 13 instead of 19, and ten of those 13 are sounds that show it with no effect on them.

Long files

Everything above is measured on sounds of 6 and 9 seconds, which is also what the reverb model learned from. Most real files are longer, so I made 134 long test sounds of 18 to 48 seconds, with and without reverb, and found a fault that every build so far has had: when the app looks at a whole long sound, it reads a room into it far too easily. Before the repair this build showed REVERB on 40 of 100 long sounds without any reverb. Twelve of them are plain clean clips in a row, and on those build 0.4.4 showed it on 9 and this build at first on 10.

A sound of more than 9 seconds is now judged in pieces of 6 seconds, each looked at as a sound of its own. It counts as having a room when the pieces say so on average, or when the last piece does, because the end is where a sound rings out. That brings the false REVERB on the long sounds from 40 to 17 of 100, and on the twelve clean ones to three, each of which ends on a sound that rings by itself. Of 100 long sounds with a real reverb 74 are shown, where it was 76. Sounds of up to 9 seconds are judged exactly as before, so nothing else on this page changes.

I also scored for the first time how well the reverb comes out of a long file, on 53 long sounds with a reverb, of which 43 get a REVERB group. A heavy reverb comes out about as well as on the short test sounds. A light reverb, where the sound itself is 5 dB or more above its reverb, does not. Of 32 long sounds with a light reverb, six come out worse than untouched with Amount half way left, and 17 with Amount at far left; on the short test sounds that was one of 16 and five of 16. Length is not the main reason. With a light reverb the engine can take more out of the sound itself than the reverb put in, and it gains most where the sound rings out at the end. A short test sound is one third ring-out and a long file is nearly all sound, so the scores of short sounds on this page flatter the engine there. Working through a long file in pieces was tried and does not repair it. Build 0.4.4 was worse on the same long sounds: ten of the 53 came out worse than untouched at half way left, where this build has six. Until the engine is better there, half way left is the safer setting for a light reverb, and far left is not.

What is not good yet. On cut-off files and excerpts the engine still does not see the room on about a third of the real reverbs, light ones most; those files stay as they are. Amount at far left is still a risk on every kind of file and makes about a fifth to a quarter of the cases worse than untouched, where half way left makes one or two of 40 worse. Sounds that ring by themselves still read as rooms. And widening, distortion and ring modulation, which have no engine of their own yet, can still make a REVERB that is not there. A larger model is the next lever for the first two. And light reverbs, as described under long files, are now the weakest spot of the reverb engine.
