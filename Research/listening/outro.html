Fewer effects that are not there: also new in build 0.4.2

Up to build 0.4.1 the app showed an effect as soon as one short stretch of the sound looked like it. That put 1.6 effects per sound on the screen that were not there. Now every effect is checked against the whole sound before it is shown. On test sounds it was not tuned on, 93 of 100 clean sounds come out with none of those effects shown; before it was 12 of 100. On the 136 test cases with an outside effect the groups shown that are not the case's effect went from 156 to 12, leaving REVERB aside.

The price: flanger, phaser, filter sweep and a fixed filter are found less often than before, because most of their earlier finds came with as many false ones. The flanger has an engine of its own since build 0.4.3, and chorus, phaser and filter sweep since build 0.4.4 (both above). The fixed filter is still to come. Turning Sureness down shows more, turning it up shows less, and it now means the same for every effect.

REVERB also showed less often where there is none. On a sound that is cut off while it is still playing, the reverb engine used to read a room into nearly half of the dry test sounds. Build 0.4.2 patched that with a second look with silence after the sound, which a cut sound with a real reverb paid for. Build 0.4.5 replaces that patch with a model that has learned such files (above).

What comes next

Next come the effect types that have no engine of their own yet: stereo widening, ring modulation, bitcrush, distortion and saturation, the exciter, stutter and the fixed filter. Once the app can take those out, about half of the REVERB that an effect still makes should go with them. The phaser gets its next step alongside, as described above. For the reverb engine two repairs are noted from this round: reading a room's decay band by band without carrying the low end's reading upward, and a second part of the model that has met wrong numbers while learning. A larger model for the reverbs it does not see on cut-off files and excerpts comes after that.
