Flat tops rebuilt: new in build 0.5.2

This is the declipper you picked. Build 0.5.1 could read the curve a sound was pushed through and walk it back, but it said of itself that tops which were pressed flat stay flat: where a curve pressed many levels into one, nothing in a value says how high it stood. The sound around a flat top does say it. The new engine makes up what each top was from the sound before and after it, and the far left of DISTORTION, and of SATURATION, now gives the rebuilt sound. Nothing was added to the screen: the same group, the same fader.

The app looks at each half of the wave by itself and tells three things apart. Tops cut off: many values sit at exactly one level, as after a clipper or an overloaded input. Nothing under the cut is touched, and the values at the cut are rebuilt above it. This is looked for on every sound, and such a sound shows DISTORTION whatever the old reading says. Tops pressed flat by a curve: the values pile up just under the top, on a sound where a bend was found. The curve is walked back as far as it can be read and the tops are rebuilt. The rebuilt tops then tell how the curve runs further up, and with the whole curve known the tops are rebuilt a second time. Neither: the sound goes the way of build 0.5.1, to the last bit.

A rebuilt top is made up, so the engine checks itself before it lets one out. It first tries its hand on the sound in front of it: it cuts tops that are whole, rebuilds them and compares. A dense, noise-like sound fails that test, because nothing in it says what its tops were. Such a sound is left as it is: left of the middle the fader then does nothing, and the fader's tip says so. Four more checks stop a rebuild that would do harm: a sound cut almost to a square wave is left alone, both halves of a wave are taken the same way, a first pass that contradicts itself is thrown out, and so is a second pass that leaves the tops rough. The group's picture says in words what was found on the sound in hand, and how high the rebuilt tops stand.

Of its 162 sounds 63 are rebuilt: 54 of them gain 5 points or more, 5 lose 5 or more, and the average gain is 53. The other 99 are not rebuilt and come out as in build 0.5.1.

The scoreboard. Of the 136 cases two change, the two distortions whose tops are pressed flat: they are examples 53 and 54 below. Taken out they now score 80 and 54, where build 0.5.1 scored −10 and 29. The other 134 cases score as they did in build 0.5.1. Over the seven distortions that the app shows on the scoreboard, taking the effect out now scores 27 on average (10 before): five with a gain and two a little worse than left alone (−7 and −11). Clean sounds are not taken for clipped more often than before: of 198 clean test sounds four show DISTORTION or SATURATION, the same four as in build 0.5.1.

The level. The rebuilt sound keeps the file's level: no value comes out higher than the file's highest was. Most clipped files are cut at full scale, and their tops must not come back over it. So the rebuilt sound is quieter than the clipped one, by about as much as its tops were cut.

On a second sound. A curve with pressed tops goes onto a second sound as in build 0.5.1. A cut whose tops were rebuilt goes on as a cut, and the second sound is pushed as far past it as the first sound's rebuilt tops stand. On the six test cases of that kind the second sound scores 72 on average (between 32 and 100), where build 0.5.1 scored 13.

What the declipper cannot do

Most distortions and saturations are not rebuilt. 56 of the 84 test sounds come out exactly as in build 0.5.1. Their effect is more than a curve, with a filter before or behind it or with plain sound mixed in, or it bends softly, and leaves no flat top to rebuild. Example 50 above is one of them. Dense sounds are left alone (example 55), and so is a sound that was clipped almost to a square wave. A wave that was flat-topped before it was clipped can come back too tall. One such case came up in the trials and is caught by a check, but the kind cannot be told from the sound. A clipper that works at a higher sample rate inside is rebuilt less often and gains less; example 56 is one, and the case of the trial that loses most. A sound that was bent only lightly can come out a little under the old way. A long file is judged by its three most clipped seconds, and a file of more than 20 minutes is not rebuilt. Cut levels that differ between left and right are not handled. A few clipped peaks, with less than half a dB to rebuild, do not show DISTORTION by themselves.

A cut whose tops are not rebuilt, on a second sound. How hard the first sound was driven is then not known. The second sound gets the curve as build 0.5.1 read it, and on a dense sound that reading is 6 to 9 dB too deep. On three of the six test cases of that kind the second sound comes out far worse than untouched, exactly as it did in build 0.5.1. This build does not mend that.

Waiting. Rebuilding takes time when a file is dropped: for a plain cut about a tenth of the sound's length, for pressed tops about a quarter of a long sound's length (a 48-second file: about 12 seconds), and a very short sound can take longer than it lasts (one second of sound: a second and a half). The thresholds of all the checks rest on about 250 test sounds, which is not many.
