Fixed filter, distortion and saturation: new in build 0.5.1

Three groups get an engine of their own in this build: FILTER, for a low-pass, a high-pass or a band-pass that stands still while the sound plays, and DISTORTION and SATURATION. A fourth, EXCITER, was tested and is taken out of the app. Up to build 0.5.0 the app could tell that a filter or a distortion was probably on a sound and little more: what it then took out, or put on a second sound, was a stand-in with settings of its own making. The new engines read the effect itself off the sound: a filter's curve in 104 bands, and for distortion and saturation the curve that every value of the sound was pushed through.

What each engine does, and what it cannot do

FILTER. A filter leaves no mark that a sound could not have by itself: a sound without highs may be a filtered sound or a dull one. What the engine goes by is that sounds are not built the way filters are. A filter's skirt falls by the same number of dB in every frame, loud or quiet, and a sound's own spectrum moves from frame to frame. Two small readers each give the filter's curve in 104 bands, and the engine takes the smaller of the two readings in every band, because a cut that is read too deep does harm when it is undone. The group is named after what was found: LOW-PASS FILTER, HIGH-PASS FILTER or BAND-PASS FILTER. Amount takes the filter out, by lifting what it cut by up to 40 dB, or puts it on a second time. Cutoff moves the curve two octaves each way, and Resonance, there when the curve has a peak, scales the peak. The picture shows the curve as read, and with a fader moved the curve that results.

On 32 test cases from four outside filters, the eight of the table and 24 more, the group is shown on 22 where the Sureness slider stands at first and on 28 with the slider down. Over the 22, taking the filter out scores 50 on average and makes no case worse than leaving it alone. Putting it on a second sound scores 63, and one case comes out worse than untouched: a high-pass whose corner is read 0.6 of an octave too high.

What it cannot do. Beyond a point a filtered sound is not told from one that is dull or thin by itself, and that goes both ways. A filter that is there is missed, or its corner is read too high or too low, where the sound has little near the corner: 10 of the 32 cases are not shown at first, 4 not at all. A filter that is not there is shown on none of the 198 clean test sounds and on 9 of the 624 test sounds of the other effect types, mostly a high-pass on a sound that has little low end. Where the filter left nothing of the sound, its curve is carried on and not read. Not read at all: a shelf or a bell of an equaliser, a notch, a filter on one side only, and a sound of under a second. A filter that moves stays with the FILTER SWEEP group.

DISTORTION and SATURATION. The two are one thing at two strengths: a fixed curve that every value of the sound is pushed through. Small values pass in a line, large ones are squeezed. The engine reads that curve off the sound, for the values above zero and for those below, because a tube or a tape does not bend the two alike. It can do that because a curve leaves the quiet frames of a sound as they were and presses the loud ones together: the same sound shows its own shape in the quiet frames and the bent shape in the loud ones. The group is called DISTORTION when the loudest values were squeezed by 10 dB or more, and SATURATION under that. It has one fader. On a second sound, Amount pushes that sound as far into the curve as the first sound was pushed: less far to the left, further to the right. On the sound itself, right pushes it further into the same curve and left walks the bend back. The picture shows the curve, what goes in against what comes out.

Blind on 96 sounds through eight outside effects, four that distort and four that saturate, the squeeze is read 1.8 dB off on average and the name is right on 85. Shown where the slider stands at first: 45 of the 48 distorted sounds, 21 of the 25 that are saturated by 6 dB or more, and 3 of the 23 that are saturated by less. On the scoreboard's eight saturation cases that is three: they go onto a second sound at 45, 61 and 85. Of its eight distortion cases seven are shown. On a second sound six of them score between 34 and 95, and one comes out a little worse than untouched (−6): a saturation plugin driven hard, whose effect is more than a curve. Taken out, three of them come out a little worse than left alone (−7, −10 and −11): that is the first limit below.

What it cannot do. A hard bend cannot be undone. Where the curve pressed many levels into one, nothing in the sound says which was which, so tops that were pressed flat stay flat. Far left puts the level relations right as far as that goes, and scores about 20 on average over the 24 cases of the blind test on which DISTORTION is shown. It does not fill the tops in. That would take a declipper, which makes up what the tops were from the sound around them: another engine, and not in this build. The way back is careful on purpose. The reading is off by a dB or two, and spreading values apart that were never squeezed is a worse fault than leaving some of the bend in, so far left takes three quarters of what was read, less 1 dB. A soft saturation is mostly not shown (3 of 23 sounds): it changes too little of the wave's shape to be told from the sound itself. A square wave or a pulse wave reads as a distorted sound, because that is what a sine pushed hard through a curve looks like. An effect that is more than a curve, with a filter before or behind it or with plain sound mixed in, is read less well, and under a loud room or behind a low-pass at 1 kHz the squeeze reads too low. Of the 198 clean test sounds 4 are shown, all with flat tops of their own, and of 608 sounds through other effects 12.

EXCITER: tested, and taken out. An exciter adds highs to a sound: partly the highs it had, made louder, partly new ones. Up to build 0.5.0 the app could show an EXCITER group. It came up on one of the eight test cases, and what it then put on the second sound was a made-up exciter that left that sound far worse than untouched. For this build I tried to give it an engine like the others, and it cannot be done from one file. A reader taught for exactly this found 8 of 40 excited sounds, the heavy-handed ones, and took 2 of the same 40 sounds before the exciter for excited. A sound with 8 dB more highs than it had is, to anything that looks at that sound alone, a bright sound. So the group is gone. With two sounds, TONE MATCH carries the first sound's highs over to the second, which is what an exciter leaves in a spectrum.

The rest of the scoreboard. The new groups come up on two cases that are not theirs. One of the eight exciter cases is an exciter built from an overdrive on the highs, mixed under the sound. The app now shows DISTORTION there. It reads the mix as one curve, which it is not, and what it then does is worse than leaving the sounds alone (−13 taken out, −29 on a second sound). On one delay case it shows SATURATION as well, which costs that case a little when everything is taken out (39, where it was 49) and nothing to speak of on a second sound (74 as before). With the EXCITER group gone, two cases that carried one that was not there, a phaser and a tremolo, no longer have their second sound made far worse by it. The other 101 cases of the 13 remaining effect types come out exactly as in build 0.5.0.
