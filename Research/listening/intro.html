This is the V2 reverb engine as it runs since build 0.4.1, with the tone engine and the echo engine of build 0.4.2 further down. After them comes what is new in build 0.4.3, the engines for level movement (tremolo, auto pan, gated rhythm) and for the flanger, then what is new in build 0.4.4, the engines for chorus, phaser and filter sweep (examples 18 to 23), then what is new in build 0.4.5: the reverb engine on files that stop while the sound plays, and fewer reverbs shown that are not there (examples 24 to 28), then what is new in build 0.4.6: the Amount fader of REVERB, and a reverb model that has learned to leave more of the sound itself alone (examples 29 to 32), then what is new in build 0.4.7: engines for Haas width, stereo width, ring modulation, bitcrush and stutter (examples 33 to 39), and last what is new in build 0.5.0: the MOVEMENT group you asked for, with its five faders Pitch, Loudness, Tone, Stereo and Spectral (examples 40 to 47). The reverb engine hears one sound and decides, band by band and moment by moment, what is room and what is the sound itself. From that it reads the room (decay time, level, width) and can turn the room down or up on that sound, or put the same room on a second sound.

Every reverb here comes from an effect that is not my code, and the engine never gets the dry sound. Examples 1 to 6 and the first table are as build 0.4.1 made them; what builds 0.4.5 and 0.4.6 change in the reverb engine is in the two sections before the last. One thing to know before you listen: since build 0.4.6 the engine's own answer is at Amount far left. Up to build 0.4.5 it sat at half way left, with a bolder end beyond it that is now gone. Headphones help.
